## Qualitative and quantitative separation

- **Qualitative:** metaphors, parables, domain intent, semantic associations, cultural/linguistic observations, and design principles.
- **Quantitative:** formulas, counters, matrices, entropy estimates, hashes, benchmark outputs, timing, memory, and pass/fail tests.
- **Bridge:** a qualitative claim may become quantitative only when a measurable variable, method, dataset, and validation criterion are named.

## VOID policy

Use `VOID` when any of these occur:

1. The archive has a term but no definition.
2. A formula is listed but no data domain is specified.
3. A metaphor suggests a mechanism but no measurable contract exists.
4. A security/privacy claim lacks a threat model.
5. A performance claim lacks benchmark conditions.
6. A hardware claim lacks architecture, ABI, or compiler details.
7. A language/cognition claim lacks evidence or scope.

`VOID` is not failure. It is the honest placeholder that prevents invented certainty.
